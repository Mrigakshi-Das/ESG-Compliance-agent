01

The problem

Cement plants sit under two rising regulatory pressures — BEE's carbon/energy scheme (PAT/CCTS) and SEBI's ESG disclosure mandate (BRSR/BRSR Core). Each reporting cycle, someone has to determine which requirements actually apply, whether the plant's data supports a compliant position, what evidence backs it, and — when it doesn't — what's driving the gap and what to fix first.

That assessment is almost always done by hand, under deadline pressure, by pulling from systems that were never built to talk to each other. Non-compliance isn't usually invisible — it's late: discovered close to a filing or audit date, when remediation is more expensive and there's no time left to investigate the real cause properly.

02

Why the existing approach fails

A typical cycle: plant data is exported to spreadsheets and emailed to a corporate sustainability analyst, who manually consolidates it, re-enters KPI formulas (inconsistently, across plants and analysts), and checks applicability against regulations from memory or a consultant. Evidence documents get chased separately by email, often discovered missing or expired only during audit prep. Root-cause analysis for any flagged issue relies on a senior engineer just knowing what happened at that plant.

Concrete failure mode

In this project's own demo data, Plant B's thermal energy consumption rose for six straight quarters before it would show up as a stark number in an annual review — an annual-cadence manual process finds this kind of slow degradation late, by design, not by accident.

03

The agent solution

Given one natural-language objective, the system plans a task-specific sequence of tool calls, retrieves plant data and regulatory requirements, runs deterministic calculations, classifies compliance status, investigates root causes, and produces a prioritized, sourced recommendation — autonomously, and reproducibly, every time.

No number is ever LLM-generated. Calculations are pure Python functions; regulatory facts come only from a sourced knowledge base; and where a fact genuinely can't be confirmed (e.g. a plant's legal designation under PAT), the system says Human Review Required rather than guessing. This is deliberate — see the 60-second case for why this makes it an agent, not a chatbot, below.

07

Limitations

Stated plainly, not buried: synthetic data (3 plants, not a real deployment); 10 regulatory requirements covered, not the full universe a real plant answers to; no live SAP or IoT/CEMS integration yet; two categories of finding structurally require human review (PAT/CCTS legal designation, BRSR company ownership) because the facts needed don't exist in this dataset; and the regulatory knowledge base needs a periodic human-owned update process as rules change.

60 seconds: why this is an agent, not a chatbot

A chatbot takes text in, text out — no memory of a task, no plan, no decision it's accountable for. This system, given one question, independently plans a multi-step task: a different plan for a single-metric question than for a full compliance assessment. It decides which of fifteen tools to call, and reacts to what they return — missing data skips a calculation instead of guessing at a number.

It carries state across the whole run: every finding labeled Fact, Assumption, or Hypothesis, never blurred together. And it shows its reasoning at the decision level — objective, plan, each tool call, each gap, each recommendation — without ever exposing a hidden chain-of-thought.

That's autonomy, planning, tool use, state, and a real feedback loop. A chatbot has none of those. This does — and you can watch it happen.
